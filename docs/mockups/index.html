Turf Review (native iPad) — UI options

Five ways to surface the review controls without the bottom-bar / narrow-column reflow. All keep the reader full-bleed and float everything else. Shots are iPad-portrait (the painful case). Tap “open live” on any card to see the raw mockup full-size.

A · Decision Dock B · Edge Rail + Drawer C · Bottom Sheet ★ D · Pencil Margin E · Pencil HUD

A · Decision Dock

Full-width reader. One floating pill always shows the recommended verdict plus a second option; ⋯ opens the full action set. Items & notes act inline on the page.

For: max reading width; the decision is unmissable; nothing ever reflows.
Against: dock floats over content; notes list is one tap away rather than always visible.

B · Edge Rail + Overlay Drawer

A thin always-on trailing rail. Tap an icon — or flick the Pencil in from the edge — and a drawer slides over the dimmed reader. The reader never resizes; closing returns full width.

For: all functions one gesture away; reader never permanently shrinks; familiar Mail/Notes feel.
Against: still a persistent ~56pt rail; overlay-over-text feels heavy if opened a lot.
open live ↗

C · Bottom Sheet with detents recommended

A native resizable sheet over a full-bleed reader (shown at the medium detent). Collapsed = a slim handle with the recommended action + quick ✓/✗; drag up for Markup / Ask / Proof. Identical in portrait & landscape.

For: smallest, most native fix; one component, one gesture; reader never horizontally squeezed; scales from a glance to full work.
Against: at the large detent it covers the reader (expected for sheets); the collapsed quick-actions must be genuinely 1-tap.

D · Pencil Margin (Books-style)

A generous gutter: handwritten notes drop as pins beside the line they anchor to, so notes are ambiently visible with no panel. The decision is a compact stack in the margin foot. Chat/Proof are toolbar popovers.

For: notes stay visible (ideal for a review tool); deeply Pencil-native; nothing covers the text.
Against: biggest build (margin-anchoring in the web doc); decision chips compete with the margin in narrow portrait.

E · Pencil HUD

The reader is 100% clean. As the Pencil approaches the screen a floating palette appears under your hand — Annotate · Highlight · Decide — and fades when you lift away. Touch users get the same HUD from the nav button.

For: most “magical” / Pencil-centric; reader is fully clean when not marking up.
Against: needs Pencil-hover hardware (touch fallback required); discoverability risk; summons the core action rather than keeping it on-screen.
